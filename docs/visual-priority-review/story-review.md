# Actual-story visual review — 2026-11-06

The largest remaining gains are in staging, shot selection and physical interactions. More detailed textures would make the current mistakes more conspicuous.

Read-only audit of the **current local production stage**, separate from the new interior shape study. Captured 77 production-camera frames across all five English sample stories, then six targeted checks. No game-source changes, deployment, paid generation or live AI directing.

## Prioritized findings

| Rank | Issue and observed evidence | Why it matters | Next correction and acceptance check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · High · mise-en-scène** | **Shots repeatedly let foreground geometry overwhelm the action.** `garden_party` at 24.5 s puts Rival Bear's cropped head/body across much of the frame while he dances; at 3.5/15.5 s Bear's torso dominates a shot of Masha. `fishing_trip` at 6.5 s is blocked by a very large birch trunk; at 21.5 s a bush masks Bear during his laugh/reaction. These are production cameras, not diagnostic views. | Readable expressions and improved hand animation cannot help when the camera hides the actor or action. Repeated accidental foreground crops also destroy the sense of an intentionally directed scene. | Add **per-location occlusion geometry and dynamic actor exclusion volumes**, then validate the actor's face, hands and important prop throughout each shot. `src/compiler/camera.ts::occlusionCost` currently uses the yard's circular occluders only when `location==='yard'`; it does not test character meshes. Judge representative moving shots, not just the initial camera key. Keep deliberate over-the-shoulder framing only when the foreground silhouette supports the action. |
| **2 · High · mise-en-scène / contact** | **Bear wakes into a standing pose inside the hammock.** `bears_nap` at 24.5, 27.5 and 30.5 s leaves the cloth passing through his lower torso. The alternate view at 27.5 s confirms a real intersection, not a misleading projection. Runtime state has Bear standing at `[-11,0,2]`, the same resting area. | The set stops behaving like a physical place at the central comic beat. This is more damaging than the hammock's texture or Bear's fur quality. | Give the hammock an authored **entry, sleeping support and dismount position**. The wake/fall transition must move Bear onto clear ground before subsequent gestures. Check the whole sleeping → waking → standing sequence against cloth, trunks and branches, with both the actual story camera and a side view. |
| **3 · High · paired interaction** | **The hug buries Masha in Bear, then leaves the bodies overlapped.** In `pancake_morning` at 56 s, a side diagnostic shows Masha's face/upper body penetrating Bear's belly and his hands missing a convincing embrace. At 57.5 s, after both hug clips end, the production shot still largely conceals her inside his silhouette. The final root separation is only about `0.357` in current scene units; that number is a diagnostic, not a sufficient collision test. | The emotional payoff currently reads as intersecting models. Polishing fingers or smiles first will not make the interaction convincing. | Solve the pair as one interaction: establish a body-safe approach, lower/lean Bear appropriately, place hands on actual shoulder/back targets, then separate to a clear resting position. Validate side and front views at approach, embrace and release. Relevant starting points: `src/compiler/compile.ts` hug handling and `src/engine/motion/verbs/gesture.ts::hug`. |
| **4 · Medium–high · actor/object proportions and staging** | **Masha's table work is staged below a surface that hides her performance.** At `pancake_morning` 24.5 s she cooks with the pan grip at Y≈`0.531`, while the outdoor tabletop is Y=`0.78`. At 27.5/33.5 s the near table edge crosses her face; the pan hangs well below the top. The asset reports Masha's height as `1.0`. Hand-to-pan contact can be accurate while the larger action remains poorly staged. | The viewer sees a small actor moving a prop beside an oversized visual barrier, rather than clearly preparing food and addressing Bear. | Add an actor-specific work position: a stable step/stool or an appropriate position beside the surface, paired with a cooking pose that presents the pan and hands. Preserve the table's useful scale for Bear. Check the work surface, actor face and both hands together in the story shot; do not fix this only by zooming in. |

## Scope and limits

- Harness: `tools/animation-story-proof.html`, served locally with HMR disabled; same fixtures, compiler, assets and stage as the game. All listed assets loaded successfully; the capture reported no browser errors. Rendering configuration, timestamps, actor states and contacts are saved in [story-metadata.json](story-metadata.json) and [story-sweep.json](story-sweep.json).
- Reviewed all five English fixtures: garden visitors, pancake morning, Bear's nap, fishing trip and cozy evening. Contact sheets also exist for [Bear's nap](story-renders/bears_nap-sheet.png) and [cozy evening](story-renders/cozy_evening-sheet.png).
- This is a sampled **render/staging review**, not a continuous-motion, audio, mobile, performance or live deployment test. Three-second sampling can miss short transition defects. The extra checks narrow four specific findings; they do not establish that every other contact is correct.
- The production interior remains the existing furnished room. The separate shape study is **not integrated**; its newly refined walls/furniture are not being judged as shipped game content here.
- An initial side-view impression suggested Masha might float during shoulder carrying. A reverse diagnostic shows her lower body meeting Bear's upper back. Therefore **floating during carry is not a confirmed finding**. See [carry diagnostic](story-renders/carry-side-diagnostic.png); motion and silhouette could still merit later review.

My recommendation: fix camera visibility, hammock dismounts and the hug first; then stage Masha's work surface. Re-run these exact story beats before investing in new face meshes or textures. Those four corrections would improve the experience across existing assets, rather than merely improve still portraits.
